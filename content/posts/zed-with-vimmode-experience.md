## The Bloat Problem

I've been a VSCode user for years. It's a solid editor—but as my projects grew larger, I started noticing the slowdown. TypeScript, Vue, Node.js, Python codebases that used to feel snappy began feeling sluggish. VSCode started to feel bloated, like carrying extra weight I didn't need.

I knew there was a better option out there. I'd heard about Zed for a long time, and decided to give it a proper try recently.

## My First Encounter with Zed

I tried Zed once before, maybe a year or two ago. The performance was incredible—that was immediately obvious. But there was a catch: Vue support wasn't great at the time. As someone working primarily with Vue and TypeScript, I couldn't justify the switch. I went back to VSCode.

Fast forward to now: I needed change. The bloat was real, and I was curious to see how much Zed had improved.

## The Recent Switch: 2 Weeks with Zed

I've been using Zed for almost 2 weeks now, primarily working with Go (building [AgentGo](./agentgo/summary.md) and [GoBroke](./gobroke/summary.md)). The Go support in Zed is **incredible**—it's noticeably better than VSCode. Language intelligence, refactoring tools, everything just feels snappier.

But more importantly, I also revisited Vue and TypeScript. And honestly? **Zed has come a long way.** The Vue support is much better than my first attempt. It's not quite at VSCode's level for Vue, but it's good enough. I can be productive. The TypeScript experience is excellent across the board.

## The Vim Catalyst

Here's where things got interesting: I've been trying to learn Vim for a while now. Vim mode in VSCode is... fine, but it's just an extension. The integration feels bolted on.

Zed gives you native Vim mode out of the box. Better yet, it's **flexible and configurable**. If a keybinding doesn't exist or doesn't suit my workflow, I can add it directly to Zed's keymap configuration. The whole experience feels native, not like an afterthought.

The result? I've found myself reaching for the mouse less and less. With Vim mode in Zed, I'm slowly getting comfortable with Vim motions without feeling completely disconnected from the GUI. It's the **perfect middle ground between a traditional IDE and pure Vim**—you get the best of both worlds.

## What I Love

**Performance**: Zed is noticeably faster than VSCode, especially with large projects. It's snappy, responsive, and a pleasure to use.

**Native Vim Support**: Having Vim mode baked in (not as an extension) makes a huge difference. The integration is seamless, and the configuration options let me customize it exactly how I want.

**Flexibility**: If I'm missing a keymap or need something custom, I can just add it. No hunting for the right extension or dealing with conflicts.

## The Challenges

I won't pretend it's perfect. Vue support, while improved, still isn't quite as polished as VSCode. If you're doing heavy Vue work, VSCode might still be the better choice.

AI integration is also noticeably better in VSCode. Copilot and other AI tools feel more integrated there. Zed is catching up, but it's not quite at parity yet.

## Tips for Making the Switch

If you're thinking about switching to Zed (especially to use Vim mode), here's what I'd recommend:

**1. Train Your Vim Discipline Early**

When starting out with Vim, make a habit of **leaving the mouse far away**. Put it on the other side of your desk. Only reach for it when you have absolutely no way to do what you want. This forces you to learn the keybindings faster.

Vim is absolutely worth the effort, especially when you have it dialed in exactly to your preference. The payoff is real.

**2. Lean on Resources**

When you need something in Vim mode and don't know how to do it, start with [Zed's Vim mode documentation](https://zed.dev/docs/vim). It explains all the Vim mode features clearly.

If you're still stuck, ask an AI. It's faster and honestly quite helpful for figuring out the right keybinding or motion.

## Final Thoughts

Two weeks in, I'm genuinely impressed with Zed. Is it perfect? No. Vue support could be better, and AI integration has room to grow.

But the performance, the native Vim support, and the overall polish make it a compelling alternative to VSCode—especially if you're willing to learn Vim and you're tired of the bloat.

If you're on the fence, I'd say: try it. Start with a project in your strongest language or somethings you're trying to learn (mine was Go). Feel the performance difference. Then experiment with Vim mode and give it a real chance. It might surprise you.
